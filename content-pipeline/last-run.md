## 1. `property-dispute-mediation-thailand-act-2562` (kb-0195)

**Title:** Mediating a Phangan property dispute: the Dispute Mediation Act and compromise contracts
**faqCategory:** process

Thailand has two formal, low-cost routes to settle a property-adjacent civil dispute
(rent arrears, a contractor payment fight, a non-ownership boundary/access disagreement,
inherited-property division among heirs) short of arbitration or a full lawsuit: the
Dispute Mediation Act B.E. 2562 (2019)'s standing out-of-court mediation system, and a
Civil Procedure Code Section 20 ter pre-filing route where either party can ask the
court to appoint a conciliator before a lawsuit is even filed. Both end in a settlement
enforceable like a court judgment. The guide leads with the single most load-bearing
limitation: Section 20 of the Act excludes disputes over **ownership of immovable
property** itself — a genuine title fight still has to go to court, no matter which
mediation route is tried. The underlying legal force of any settlement comes from CCC
Sections 850-852 (compromise contracts): a compromise settles a dispute by mutual
concession (Section 850), isn't enforceable by court action without written evidence
signed by the liable party (Section 851), and extinguishes abandoned claims while
confirming retained rights (Section 852).

Sources: ThailandLawOnline's direct-fetch translation of CCC Sections 850-852 (quoted
verbatim); Mahanakorn Partners Group's direct-fetch summary of Civil Procedure Code
Section 20 ter (Amendment Act No. 32, B.E. 2563/2020, in force 7 Nov 2020 — no court fee,
court-appointed conciliator, enforceable consent judgment), cross-checked against
Tilleke & Gibbins' piece on the same amendment; the Dispute Mediation Act's Section 20
scope (ownership-of-immovable-property disputes excluded; non-ownership land disputes,
heir disputes over inherited property, and other civil disputes up to ฿5M included) via
a WebSearch-quoted excerpt of a Lexology piece (direct fetch returned HTTP 403),
independently corroborated by the research agent's own extraction of the Thai-language
gazetted Act text. Confirmed distinct via direct reads of
`arbitration-clause-thai-property-lease-disputes` (needs a pre-existing arbitration
clause, THAC-administered — a different, contract-dependent mechanism),
`damrongtham-center-land-property-complaints-thailand` (an administrative complaint
channel with no power to record a binding settlement) and
`consumer-case-procedure-act-buyer-developer-disputes` (court litigation only) — none
mention mediation, compromise contracts, or Section 20 ter.

## 2. `unmarried-cohabiting-partner-property-rights-thailand` (kb-0196)

**Title:** Buying together without marrying in Thailand: why an unmarried partner inherits nothing
**faqCategory:** ownership

Thailand recognizes no common-law or de facto marriage — only a marriage registered at
the district Amphur office (CCC Section 1457) creates a legal spouse. An unmarried
partner (opposite-sex or same-sex, regardless of years cohabiting or financial
contribution) is not a statutory heir under CCC Sections 1629/1635 and inherits nothing
automatically if the other partner dies without a will; on separation, there's no
marital-property regime to divide, only case-by-case co-ownership principles requiring
proof of actual contribution to each specific asset. The guide covers the real
protections available regardless of marital status: a Thai will (no forced-heirship rule
blocks naming a partner as legatee), a registered usufruct/superficies/habitation right
granted directly to the partner, joint registration on a lease or company shareholding,
and a written cohabitation agreement (enforceable as an ordinary civil contract, though
it doesn't itself transfer title or substitute for a will).

Sources: thethailandlife.com and samuiforsale.com (independently consistent on no
common-law-marriage recognition and the resulting zero automatic inheritance/property
claim); pslawbusiness.com (cohabitation agreements enforceable as ordinary civil
contracts; Thai courts have applied co-ownership principles to divide jointly acquired
assets on separation, but this doesn't substitute for a will on inheritance); Thailand
Law Library (Siam Legal)'s Statutory Heirs page (CCC Sections 1635-1638, the registered
spouse's inheritance share). Confirmed distinct via direct reads of
`marriage-equality-act-property-rights-same-sex-couples` (covers only *registered*
same-sex marriages), `foreign-spouse-usufruct-thai-marriage-property` and
`thai-prenuptial-agreement-property-protection` (both marriage-dependent protections)
and `intestate-succession-statutory-heirs-thai-property` (confirms "a **registered**
spouse is a statutory heir" but never addresses unmarried partners) — plus a
full-catalog grep for "cohabit"/"common-law"/"unmarried partner" (no hits beyond an
unrelated "de facto control" clause in a company-structure guide).
